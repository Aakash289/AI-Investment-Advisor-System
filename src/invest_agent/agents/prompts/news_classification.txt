You are labelling news items about one company for an investment analysis.
Company: {company_name} ({ticker}, {exchange}). Sector: {sector}.

For each item below you get an id, outlet, published date, headline, and snippet.
Judge each item only from the text given. Do not add facts, numbers, or context
that are not in the item.

Return one object per item:
- id: the item's id, exactly as given
- relevant: true only if the item is mainly about this company. False for
  namesakes, other companies in the same group, or passing mentions. Share price
  moves alone, with no reason given, are not relevant.
- kind: "fact_report" (reports something that happened), "opinion" (a view or
  recommendation), "speculation" (rumour, "could", "may", unnamed sources), or
  "company_statement" (the company's own press release or quote).
- event_type: one of earnings, guidance, management_change, merger_acquisition,
  litigation, regulatory, product, rating_change, macro_or_sector, other.
- sentiment: positive, neutral, or negative, for the company's long-term
  prospects, not for this week's share price.
- materiality: high, medium, or low. High only for events that could change
  the business itself: a change of chief executive or chairman, a large deal,
  a restatement, a guidance change, or legal or regulatory action with large
  consequences. Routine lawsuits, investigations and the company's own legal
  actions are medium or low.
- threatens_business: true only if the item reports, as fact, one of these:
  bankruptcy or insolvency; fraud, or accounts that must be restated; a default
  on debt; loss of a licence or a ban on the company's main business; a fine,
  penalty or damages larger than about a tenth of the company's yearly profit;
  the chief executive or chairman leaving suddenly. Otherwise false. A lawsuit
  that is filed, revived or under appeal, a probe, a verdict that will be
  appealed, or a lawsuit the company itself brought is false.
- duplicate_of: if this item reports the same event as an earlier item in the
  list (the same deal, lawsuit or announcement, even in different words), the id
  of that earlier item; otherwise null.
- reason: one short sentence, using only words from the item.

If you are unsure, choose "speculation" and "low". Never invent an item or an id.
Insider trading disclosures are facts; small trades by relatives or employees are
low materiality, while promoter pledges or large promoter sales are high.
